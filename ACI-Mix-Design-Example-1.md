## Slide 2 — Problem statement

We require a mix with a mean 28-day compressive strength (measured on standard
cylinders) of **35 MPa** and a **slump of 50 mm**, ordinary Portland cement being used.
The maximum size of well-shaped, angular aggregate is **20 mm**, its bulk density is
**1600 kg/m³**, and its specific gravity is **2.64**. The available fine aggregate has a
fineness modulus of **2.60** and a specific gravity of **2.58**. **No air entrainment** is
required. For the sake of completeness, all steps, even when obvious, will be given.

---

## Slide 3 — Solution: Steps 1 and 2

**Step 1: Choice of slump.** A slump of 50 mm is specified.

**Step 2: Choice of maximum size of aggregate.** The maximum size of aggregate of
20 mm is specified.

From **Table 14.5**, for a slump of 50 mm and a maximum size of aggregate of 20 mm
(or 19 mm), the water requirement is approximately **190 kg per cubic metre** of

## Slide 6 — Step 4: selection of water/cement ratio

From past experience, a water/cement ratio of **0.48** is expected to result in concrete
with a compressive strength, measured on cylinders, of **35 MPa (5076.31)** — see
**Table 2**. There are no special durability requirements.

> When different cementitious materials are used, it should be remembered that they
> have varying values of specific gravity: typical values are **3.15 for Portland
> cement**, **2.90 for ground granulated blast furnace slag**, and **2.30 for fly ash**.

## Slide 11 — Step 7: estimate of fine aggregate content

To calculate the mass of fine aggregate, we need first to calculate the volume of all the
other ingredients. The required values are as follows:

- Volume of water is **190 / 1000 = 0.19 m³**.
- Solid volume of cement, assuming usual specific gravity of **3.15**, is
  **395 / (3.15 × 1000) = 0.126 m³**.
- Solid volume of coarse aggregate is **1020 / (2.64 × 1000) = 0.396 m³**.
- Volume of entrapped air, given in Table 14.5, is **0.02 × 1000 = 0.020 m³**.

---

## Slide 12 — Step 7 concluded

Hence, total volume of all ingredients except fine aggregate = **0.732 m³**.

Therefore, the required volume of fine aggregate is

**1.000 − 0.732 = 0.268 m³.**

Hence, the mass of fine aggregate is

**0.268 × 2.58 × 1000 = 690 kg/m³.**

From the various steps, we can list the estimated mass of each of the ingredients in
kilograms per cubic metre of concrete as follows.

## Slide 14 — Design Example 2

Design the concrete for an unreinforced, air-entrained pavement, in a very cold climate
where there is no statistical data available for the proposed mix design; **25 cm thick**;
specified 28-day concrete **flexural strength of 4.7 MPa**; the coarse aggregates have a
bulk specific gravity of **2.70**; a rodded density of **1650 kg/m³** at the saturated
surface dry (SSD) condition and a moisture content of **1.5% above the SSD condition**;
the fine aggregates have a bulk specific gravity of **2.65** with a fineness modulus of
**2.75** and a moisture of **5% above the SSD condition**. The apparent specific gravity
of the Portland cement = **3.15**.

---

## Slide 15 — Design solution: flexural strength

For structural concrete, the required compressive strength is specified. Only in the
case of pavements is the flexural strength criterion used instead. However, since the
mix design tables are predicated on compressive strength, the equation on the next
slide is used to determine the approximate equivalent compressive strength, f′c.

---

## Slide 16 — Equivalent compressive strength

- f′c = (M<sub>R</sub> / K)² — in MPa for the SI system, K = 0.7 to 0.8.
- f′c = (M<sub>R</sub> / K)² — in psi for the standard U.S. system, K = 7.5 to 10.

M<sub>R</sub> stands for modulus of rupture.

**f′c = (M<sub>R</sub> / 0.8)² = (4.7 / 0.8)² = 34.5 MPa.**

---

## Slide 17 — Step 1: required mix design strength

Determine the required mix design strength, f′cr, from **Table 7**:

**f′cr = 34.5 + 8.3 = 42.8 MPa.**

## Slide 20 — Step 3: maximum size of coarse aggregate, criteria 1–2

In selecting the maximum size of coarse aggregate, there are a number of criteria that
need to be met. They will all be enumerated here even though not all are applicable in
this illustrative design problem. The criteria for the maximum permissible size of
aggregate are as follows:

1. Not to exceed one-fifth the narrowest dimension between the insides of a form.
2. Three-quarters of the clear space between reinforcing bars, ducts, or any other
   appurtenances embedded in the concrete.

---

## Slide 21 — Step 3: criteria 3–4

3. Three-quarters of the clear space between the reinforcing bars and the inside face of
   the forms.
4. In the case of an unreinforced concrete slab, one-third the minimum slab thickness,
   where the concrete is not a uniform depth.

In the case of the 25-cm pavement thickness, this would translate to a **75-mm
aggregate**, which is on the high side, but if available and economical, should be used.
Generally speaking, a 50-mm aggregate is the largest size that is commonly
encountered.

---

## Slide 22 — Step 4: air content

The air content depends principally upon the environment under which the structure
will be functioning. Refer to **Table 10**. The structure in this problem would qualify as a
**Category 1**, exposed to freezing and thawing, for an air content of **4 to 7%**.
**Table 11** indicates a target percent of entrained air at **4.5**, which is in the 4 to 7%
range shown in Table 10. For any number of reasons it is not possible to specify and
expect to repeatedly obtain an exact % of air.

---

## Slide 23 — Step 4: target air content

Furthermore, the author is of the opinion that slightly more air is preferable to less air.
Therefore, a target percent air will be chosen of **−1% to +2%**. In the case of this
illustrative design example, a **target percent of air of 5%** (−1% to +2%) for a range of
4 to 7% was chosen.

## Slide 31 — Step 7: fine aggregate

At this point the quantity of all the materials in the mix has been accounted for except
for the fine aggregates. The latter is found by subtracting the volume of the air,
cement, coarse aggregates, and water from a cubic metre to estimate the fine aggregate
quantity in the batch.

---

## Slide 32 — Step 7 arithmetic

- Air = 0.05 = **0.050 m³**
- Cement = 349 / (3.15 × 1000) = **0.111 m³**
- Coarse aggregates = 1416 / (2.70 × 1000) = **0.524 m³**
- Water = 122 / (1 × 1000) = **0.122 m³**
- Total volume of the above materials = **0.807 m³**
- Computed volume of fine aggregates = 1.000 − 0.807 = **0.193 m³**
- Weight of fine aggregates in the concrete batch = 0.193 × 2.65 × 1000 = **511 kg**

---

## Slide 33 — Total batch weight

Total weight of all the ingredients in the 1-cubic-metre concrete batch:

**349 kg (cement) + 1416 kg (coarse aggregates) + 511 kg (fine aggregates) + 122 kg
(water) = 2398 kg.**

---

## Slide 34 — Step 8: moisture adjustment

A moisture correction at this point is needed to compensate for the moisture in the
aggregates above that present for the SSD condition. The new trial batch weights are as
follows:

- Coarse aggregates = 1416 × 1.015 = **1437 kg**
- Fine aggregates = 511 × 1.05 = **537 kg**
- Water = 122 − 1416 × 0.015 − 511 × 0.05 = **75 kg**
- Cement = **349 kg**
- Total materials after water adjustment remains the same: **2398 kg**

# Conversion notes

These notes are **not part of the source slides**. They record transcription decisions
and arithmetic checks so the document can be used safely as a test specimen.

## 1. Edition of the tables

- The source is a teaching example built on **ACI 211.1-91** tables. Slide 5 states this
  explicitly in the title of Table 14.5.
- The following values therefore differ from ACI PRC-211.1-22:
  - **Water content:** the source uses **190 kg/m³** for 19 mm aggregate / 25–50 mm
    slump, from the metric 211.1-91 table. ACI 211.1-22 Table 5.3.3 gives
    **315 lb/yd³ = 186.9 kg/m³ ≈ 187 kg/m³** for the ¾ in. column.
  - **Coarse aggregate volume:** the source's 0.64 at FM 2.60 agrees with
    ACI 211.1-22 Table 5.3.6.
  - **w/c:** the source's 0.48 at "35 MPa" is the 5000 psi row of the 1991 table.
    ACI 211.1-22 Table 5.3.4 gives 0.48 at 5000 psi (34.5 MPa) and interpolates
    to about 0.475 at 35 MPa.
  - **Air content:** 2% entrapped air at 20 mm agrees with both editions.
  - **Target strength (Example 2's Table 7):** the source uses `f′c + 1400 psi`
    (+9.6 MPa) above 5000 psi; ACI 211.1-22 Table 4.7.4.1 (ACI 301-20 Table
    4.2.3.3(b)) uses `1.10 f′c + 700 psi`. At 34.5 MPa both land on 43 MPa after
    rounding up; at 35 MPa they differ (44 vs 45).
- Do not mix the source's 1991 table values with 211.1-22 values in one check.

## 2. Arithmetic inconsistencies in the source (preserved above)

1. **Coarse aggregate mass:** 0.64 × 1600 = **1024 kg/m³**, not 1020 kg/m³. The slide
   prints 1020.
2. **Coarse aggregate solid volume:** 1020 / (2.64 × 1000) = **0.3864 m³**, not
   0.396 m³. The printed 0.396 is a transposition.
3. **Total volume except fine aggregate:** with 0.190 + 0.126 + 0.386 + 0.020 the total
   is **0.722 m³**, not 0.732 m³. The printed 0.732 follows from the 0.396 typo.
4. **Fine aggregate mass:** the correct absolute-volume closure gives
   **0.2782 × 2.58 × 1000 ≈ 718 kg/m³**, not 690 kg/m³. The printed 690 follows from
   the 0.268 m³ that came from the 0.732 m³ total; even that product is 691.4, so the
   slide also truncates.
5. **Density:** 190 + 395 + 1020 + 690 = **2295 kg/m³** is internally consistent with
   the printed masses, but those masses do not close the absolute-volume balance
   (they sum to about 0.989 m³). A balanced batch using the source's own water,
   cement and 1020 kg coarse aggregate would contain about **718 kg/m³** of fine
   aggregate and weigh about **2323 kg/m³**.
6. **"Dry" labels:** slides 13 and 11 label the aggregate masses "dry", but the
   calculation uses the **SSD** specific gravities (2.58 and 2.64) and ACI's design
   weights are SSD weights (ACI PRC-211.1-22 clause 5.3.6–5.3.8). The label is loose, not
   the calculation.

## 3. Other source inconsistencies

- Slide 6 writes "35 MPa (5076.31)"; 5076.31 is the psi equivalent of 35 MPa, not a
  second strength.
- Slide 18 says Table 9 "does not show the w/c ratio beyond a strength of 35 MPa". That is
  true for the **air-entrained** column (the 40 MPa cell is a dash); the non-air column

  "quesstimate" is still **below** the table's 0.39 air-entrained value at 35 MPa and
  below every listed air-entrained value — a mix richer and less workable than the table
  supports, not an interpolation.
- Slide 29's "minimum cement 335 kg/m³" does **not** appear in the body of the
  reproduced Table 8 (282 / 324 / 354 / 366 kg/m³ for the 40/20/14/10 mm rows); it is
  the table's footnote minimum for a very cold climate, which is exactly this example's
  exposure. The slide's 335 is correct — the body values are a different row set, not a
  contradiction.
- Slide 35 leaves the 10-kg batch quantities blank.
- The homework on slide 36 is not solved; slides 37 and 38 supply only the reference
  tables.

## 4. Moisture convention used by the source

Example 2 (slides 14 and 34) expresses aggregate moisture **above SSD**:

- 1416 × 1.015 = 1437 kg
- 511 × 1.05 = 537 kg
- water = 122 − 1416 × 0.015 − 511 × 0.05 = 75 kg

That is **free moisture as a percentage of the SSD mass** (0% = SSD). It is the
convention used by the app's input labels, and it is *not* the same as the total
moisture content of ASTM C566 that appears in the ACI 211.1-22 clause 5.3.9.1 formula.
The distinction matters when testing the app; see the accompanying test report.
